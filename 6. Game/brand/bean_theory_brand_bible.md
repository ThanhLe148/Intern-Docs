# Bean Theory Coffee, Brand Bible

## Company Snapshot
Bean Theory is a specialty coffee brand founded in 2021 by Maya Chen, a former physicist turned cafe owner. The "theory" angle is real: every drink on the menu is named after a scientific concept (Quantum Espresso, Friction Latte, Gravity Cold Brew). Two locations open in Toronto, a third coming Q2.

## Founder
- **Maya Chen**, 34, ex-University of Toronto physics PhD
- Lives in Leslieville, Toronto
- Believes coffee should be precise, repeatable, and a little nerdy
- Hates the word "artisanal"

## Locations
- **Location 1:** Queen Street West (flagship, 2021)
- **Location 2:** Leslieville (2023)
- **Location 3:** The Junction (opening Q2 of current year)

## Mission
Make scientifically excellent coffee feel approachable. Every cup should taste the same whether you visit on a Monday or a Sunday.

## Voice and Tone
- Smart but not smug
- Warm, slightly nerdy, conversational
- Uses science metaphors sparingly, not constantly
- Never uses "artisanal," "handcrafted," "curated," "elevated"
- Avoids exclamation marks
- Writes in second person when speaking to customers

## Sample Voice
**Yes:**
> "Our espresso is pulled at 9 bars and 93 degrees. We measure everything because consistency is the point."

**No:**
> "Experience our handcrafted, artisanal espresso, lovingly curated by our passionate baristas!"

## Mascot
**Bruno the Bean** is a stylized coffee bean character with safety goggles and a small lab coat. Used in the loyalty game (Project 01) and across kid-friendly merch.

## Drink Naming Convention
Every drink is named after a physics concept. Examples:
- Quantum Espresso (single shot)
- Entropy Cortado
- Friction Latte (extra foam)
- Gravity Cold Brew
- Photon Pour-Over
- Half-Life Decaf

## Customer Persona
- Tech and design professionals 25 to 45
- People who like specificity over vibes
- Toronto residents who walk to their coffee shop
- Curious learners, not status seekers

## Do's
- Talk about temperature, pressure, ratios when it adds clarity
- Show the science when it makes the coffee better
- Use plain language for newcomers, depth for regulars
- Credit suppliers and farms by name

## Don'ts
- No exclamation marks in marketing copy
- No emoji in formal communications
- No "we are passionate about coffee" cliches
- No vague aesthetic language ("vibes," "feels," "energy")

## Reusable Phrases
- "We measure everything because consistency is the point."
- "Coffee, but with the variables controlled."
- "Same cup. Every time. By design."
